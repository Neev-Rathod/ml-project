## Feature Extraction

### Method

An **84-dimensional feature vector** is extracted from each 10-second, 100 Hz, 12-lead waveform (shape 1000 × 12). Features are split into two equal views for WP4's Co-Training.

#### View 1 — Time-Domain / Morphological (indices 0–41, 42 features)

| Feature Group | Features | Description |
|---------------|----------|-------------|
| Per-lead mean & std | 24 (2 × 12 leads) | Amplitude statistics per ECG lead |
| HRV from Lead II | 3 | Mean RR interval, Std RR, RMSSD (root mean square successive differences) |
| QRS amplitude range | 1 | max − min across all 12 leads |
| Zero-crossing rate | 1 | Mean ZCR across all leads |
| Skewness / Kurtosis | 2 | Signal distribution shape (mean over leads) |
| Hjorth parameters | 3 | Activity, Mobility, Complexity from Lead II |
| Padding | 8 | Zero-padded to reach exactly 42 |

#### View 2 — Frequency-Domain / Wavelet (indices 42–83, 42 features)

| Feature Group | Features | Description |
|---------------|----------|-------------|
| Welch PSD band powers | 36 (3 bands × 12 leads) | Power in 0.5–4 Hz, 4–15 Hz, 15–40 Hz |
| Spectral entropy | 1 | Mean Shannon entropy of PSD across leads |
| Spectral roll-off | 1 | Mean frequency below which 85% of PSD energy falls |
| Wavelet sub-band energies | 4 | db4 DWT detail coefficients L1–L4 from Lead II |

### Why These Features?

- **HRV statistics** capture heart rate regularity — a strong discriminator between NORM and pathological classes.
- **Hjorth parameters** are computationally cheap proxies for signal complexity; Complexity rises in arrhythmic patterns.
- **PSD band powers** separate low-frequency baseline wander (0.5–4 Hz) from clinically meaningful QRS/T-wave content (4–40 Hz).
- **Wavelet energies** capture transient localized features (Q-waves, ST changes) that Fourier methods smear in time.

### Data Quality

- Feature matrix shape: **21,837 × 84**, dtype float32.
- **1 record** had NaN in skewness/kurtosis (caused by a flat-line lead with zero standard deviation). Imputed with the column median.
- No other NaN or Inf values.

## Sensor Noise & Subspace Analysis Results

### Research Question (Proposal §1.3, Q1)

> *How do physical sensor corruptions distort standard distance metrics, and how effectively do PCA and Mahalanobis scaling restore cluster quality?*

### Method

k-Means clustering (k = 5, matching the 5 diagnostic classes) was run on training records under 6 variants:

- **Raw vs. PCA** (top 20 components)
- **Distance metric:** Euclidean, Cosine, Mahalanobis

Cluster purity was measured separately for:
- All training records
- Clean records only (no `baseline_drift` or `static_noise` flag)
- Noisy records (at least one artifact flag set)

### Results

| Variant | Purity (All) | Purity (Clean) | Purity (Noisy) | ARI (All) |
|---------|-------------|----------------|----------------|-----------|
| Raw + Euclidean | 0.5778 | 0.5910 | 0.5400 | 0.0486 |
| Raw + Cosine | 0.5589 | 0.5696 | 0.5332 | 0.0416 |
| Raw + Mahalanobis | 0.5646 | 0.5720 | 0.5359 | **0.1081** |
| PCA + Euclidean | 0.5777 | 0.5863 | 0.5442 | 0.0723 |
| PCA + Cosine | 0.5589 | 0.5713 | 0.5332 | 0.0427 |
| PCA + Mahalanobis | **0.5771** | **0.5857** | **0.5438** | 0.0814 |

### Interpretation

**1. Sensor artifacts consistently hurt clustering.**
In every variant, noisy records (with `baseline_drift` or `static_noise`) have lower cluster purity than clean records. The largest gap is in Raw + Euclidean (0.591 clean vs. 0.540 noisy — a 5 pp drop), confirming the proposal's hypothesis that sensor noise distorts Euclidean distances.

**2. Mahalanobis distance provides the best ARI on raw features.**
Raw + Mahalanobis achieves ARI = 0.108, more than double the Raw + Euclidean ARI of 0.049. This is because Mahalanobis scaling whitens the feature covariance, reducing the influence of correlated, high-variance noise dimensions (baseline drift affects multiple leads simultaneously and creates correlated features).

**3. PCA narrows the clean–noisy purity gap.**
PCA + Euclidean reduces the clean-noisy gap to 4.2 pp (vs. 5.1 pp for Raw + Euclidean), showing that PCA partially discards the noise dimensions by keeping only the highest-variance signal subspace.

**4. The absolute purity values (~0.56–0.59) reflect genuine class overlap.**
PTB-XL's five classes are not cleanly separable in a 20-component PCA subspace — MI and STTC share similar ST-segment patterns, and NORM vs. HYP can appear similar in lead II. This motivates the need for semi-supervised methods that use geometry more carefully (WP3) or probabilistic label modeling (WP5).

**5. Recommended configuration for WP3 (Graph Label Propagation).**
Based on these results, WP3 should use **Mahalanobis distance** for graph construction (highest ARI = 0.108), with PCA pre-processing to 20 components to reduce computation cost.

## PCA Scree Plot Interpretation

The scree plot (`pca_explained_variance.png`) shows:
- The first 5 PCA components explain approximately 60% of variance.
- Reaching 90% cumulative variance requires roughly 30–35 components.
- The curve flattens after component 20, suggesting that 20 components is a reasonable trade-off for WP3's graph construction.

This confirms the proposal's design choice to use PCA as a pre-processing step before computing nearest-neighbor graphs.

## Limitations and Notes for Other WPs

1. **R-peak detection is threshold-based.** The `_rr_intervals()` function uses a simple 60% threshold rather than a full Pan-Tompkins detector. HRV features (indices 24–26) may be inaccurate for very noisy or low-amplitude leads. WP4 should treat these features with caution.

2. **Multi-label records are excluded.** 5,565 records (25.5%) with ambiguous or multi-class labels were dropped. This likely under-represents borderline cases, which are exactly the records that semi-supervised methods need to handle. WP5's EM may benefit from treating these as fully unlabeled.

3. **Class imbalance.** NORM dominates both splits (62.5% of D_L). All WPs must use macro-averaged F1 as the primary metric (not accuracy) and should consider class-balanced training or sampling.

4. **Gold Set size.** With 9,010 labeled records, η = 100% of D_L is a relatively generous starting point. The interesting regime for WP3–WP5 is η ∈ {2%, 5%, 10%} of D_L, which corresponds to 180, 450, and 900 labeled records respectively.
